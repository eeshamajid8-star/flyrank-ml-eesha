Content Refresh Opportunity Scoring:
A Data-Driven Priority Engine

Eesha Majid  ·  eeshamajid8@gmail.com  ·  October 2026  ·  General AI Fluency + Machine Learning Track
Abstract

Content editors managing large websites face a prioritization problem: they cannot refresh all pages, so they need a data-driven ranked list. This paper asks: which content pages are most worth refreshing based on their current search performance signals? Using 30,000 anonymized content pages from the FlyRank ML Internship dataset, we build and validate a Random Forest classifier that predicts declining pages and ranks them by refresh priority. Our model achieves Precision@20 of 0.950 compared to 0.500 for a rule-based baseline — meaning 19 out of every 20 top-flagged pages are genuinely declining. The output is a ranked action playbook with three tiers: REFRESH_NOW, REVIEW_SOON, and MONITOR. All findings are observational and directional; we make no causal claims about refresh impact on ranking.

Section 01

Introduction & Problem Statement

A content editor at a large website cannot refresh every page every week. With thousands of pages in a portfolio, the decision of where to focus editorial effort is typically driven by intuition, staleness dates, or simple rules like "refresh pages older than 6 months." These approaches ignore the most relevant signal: actual search performance.

This work builds a scoring model that ranks pages by refresh priority using observable search signals — impressions, CTR, position, and trend direction. The output supports a concrete decision: which pages to put in the content editor's queue this week.

The Decision This Work Supports

A content editor opens the top-ranked pages from this model's output and schedules them for a content update. Pages ranked lower stay in the backlog. The cost of a wrong recommendation is wasted editor time — spending a week refreshing a page that did not need it, while the pages that actually needed attention keep declining.

Section 03

Methodology

Label Definition

trend_direction == 'down' → positive class (page is declining in impressions). This is a proxy label — we observe performance decline as a signal for refresh need. We cannot directly observe "needs refresh."

Features Used

impressions_90d, ctr, avg_position, content_age_days, clicks_90d, sessions_90d, scroll_rate, engagement_rate. All are 90-day lookback signals — no future window used.

Baseline Rule

Score = impressions_90d × (1 − ctr). Higher score = higher refresh priority. Simple, interpretable, no training required.

Model

Random Forest Classifier — 100 trees, max depth 5. Chosen for interpretability via feature importance and robustness on tabular data.

Leakage Check

trend_direction used only as label, never as a feature. No product flags or future-window columns included. Score formula verified: uses only historical lookback signals.

Section 05

Limitations & Honest Framing

  • Cannot claim causation — refreshing a page may not improve its ranking. This model identifies correlation, not cause.
  • trend_direction is a proxy label — it measures impression change, not content quality. A page can decline for reasons unrelated to content.
  • Zero-impression pages excluded — pages with no search visibility are a different problem not addressed here.
  • Single ecosystem — dataset covers one client portfolio. Patterns may not generalise to all website types or industries.
  • Algorithm changes — external ranking changes can cause decline signals unrelated to content quality.

All findings are observational and directional. This model supports a decision — it does not replace editorial judgment.
